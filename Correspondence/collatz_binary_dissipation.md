## Abstract

We introduce a binary structural framework for analysing the Collatz transformation. Rather than studying arithmetic growth of iterates, we examine how the transformation acts on the *informational geometry* of binary representations. Three structural quantities are defined: the bit-length $L(n)$, the Hamming weight $H(n)$, and the cluster connectivity $C(n)$, counting adjacent $\mathtt{11}$ pairs. From these we derive the **clustering ratio**

$$R(n) = \frac{C(n)}{H(n)},$$

a normalised measure of structural aggregation. We establish several rigorous properties of these quantities under the Collatz map, including an exact identity for $H(3n+1)$, a carry propagation theorem, and an MSB ejection lemma showing that the fully-clustered state $R = 1$ is unreachable. Together these results suggest that the Collatz transformation acts as a **deterministic binary dissipation engine**, progressively destroying cluster connectivity. We propose the clustering ratio as a candidate Lyapunov observable and outline a programme of open problems whose resolution would establish convergence.

## 1. Introduction

The Collatz conjecture asserts that for every positive integer $n$, repeated application of

$$T(n) = \begin{cases} n/2 & n \text{ even} \\ (3n+1)/2^{v_2(3n+1)} & n \text{ odd} \end{cases}$$

eventually reaches the cycle $1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 1$, where $v_2(k)$ denotes the 2-adic valuation of $k$. Despite its elementary statement, the conjecture remains open. Most existing approaches study arithmetic growth or 2-adic properties of the iterates directly.

We adopt a different perspective: the Collatz map is reinterpreted as a **transformation on binary structures**, and convergence is studied through the evolution of structural quantities defined on bitstring representations. This perspective is motivated by two observations:

1. The Collatz map in binary acts via carry propagation (odd branch) and right-shift (even branch) — both are local bitstring operations.
2. Carry propagation through clusters of $\mathtt{1}$-bits is a destructive operation: it converts structured clusters into dispersed configurations.

This suggests that the "chaos" of Collatz trajectories may be reinterpreted as a systematic **dissipation of binary structure**, analogous to entropy production in thermodynamic systems.

The present paper establishes the foundational structural lemmas and proposes the clustering ratio $R(n)$ as a candidate Lyapunov function. We make no claim of proving the conjecture; our aim is to provide a rigorous framework and a precise programme of open problems.

## 2. Binary Structural Measures

Let $n$ be a positive integer with binary representation

$$n = \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} b_i \, 2^i, \qquad b_i \in \{0,1\},\ b_{L-1} = 1.$$

We define the following structural quantities.

**Definition 2.1 (Bit-length).** $L(n) = \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1$.

**Definition 2.2 (Hamming weight).** $H(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} b_i$, the number of $\mathtt{1}$-bits.

**Definition 2.3 (Cluster connectivity).** 
$$C(n) = \mathrm{Hamming}\!\left((n \gg 1)\ \&\ n\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{L-1} b_i \, b_{i-1},$$
the number of adjacent $\mathtt{11}$ pairs in the binary representation.

**Definition 2.4 (Clustering ratio).** For $H(n) > 0$,
$$R(n) = \frac{C(n)}{H(n)} \in [0, 1).$$

**Definition 2.5 (Cluster count).** A *cluster* is a maximal contiguous block of $\mathtt{1}$-bits. We denote by $m(n)$ the number of clusters, and by $k_1 \leq k_2 \leq \cdots \leq k_m$ their sizes (from LSB to MSB), so that $\sum_j k_j = H(n)$.

The extreme cases of $R$ are informative:

| Configuration | Example | $R$ |
|---|---|---|
| Single dense cluster | $\mathtt{11111}$ | $(H-1)/H \to 1$ |
| Alternating string | $\mathtt{10101}$ | $0$ |
| Isolated $\mathtt{1}$-bits | $\mathtt{10001}$ | $0$ |

Thus $R$ measures how strongly the $\mathtt{1}$-bits are aggregated into contiguous blocks.

## 3. The Collatz Map in Binary

For odd $n$, the computation $3n + 1 = (n \ll 1) + n + 1$ can be analysed via the carry sequence generated by bitwise addition.

**Definition 3.1 (Carry sequence).** Given odd $n$ with bits $b_0, \ldots, b_{L-1}$, define:
$$c_0 = 1, \qquad c_{i+1} = \left\lfloor \frac{b_{i-1} + b_i + c_i}{2} \right\rfloor,$$
with $b_{-1} = 0$. The output bits of $3n+1$ are $r_i = (b_{i-1} + b_i + c_i) \bmod 2$.

The conservation law at each position is:

$$b_{i-1} + b_i + c_i = 2c_{i+1} + r_i. \tag{$*$}$$

The even branch $n \mapsto n/2$ acts as a pure right-shift, removing the LSB (which is $0$) and preserving all other bits.

**Observation 3.2 (Even branch preserves $C$ and $H$).** If $b_0 = 0$, then $H(n/2) = H(n)$ and $C(n/2) = C(n)$, so $R(n/2) = R(n)$. Only $L$ and $T_H = H/L$ change.

This decoupling is structurally significant: $R$ is exclusively an *odd-step observable*.

## 4. Core Structural Lemmas

### 4.1 Carry Propagation

**Lemma 4.1 (Carry-Through-Cluster).** *Let $b_a = b_{a+1} = \cdots = b_{a+k-1} = 1$ be a cluster of length $k$, with incoming carry $c_a = 1$. Then $c_{a+k} = 1$.*

**Proof.** By induction on $k$. At each position $j \in [a, a+k-1]$: the inputs $b_{j-1} \geq 0$, $b_j = 1$, $c_j = 1$ satisfy $b_{j-1} + b_j + c_j \geq 2$, so $c_{j+1} = 1$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 4.2.** *A carry entering the bottom of any cluster of $\mathtt{1}$-bits exits the top. Clusters are carry-transparent.*

**Lemma 4.3 (Carry Absorption).** *If $b_i = 0$, $b_{i-1} = 0$, and $c_i = 1$, then $c_{i+1} = 0$. A carry entering a gap of two or more consecutive $\mathtt{0}$-bits is absorbed.*

**Proof.** $b_{i-1} + b_i + c_i = 0 + 0 + 1 = 1 < 2$, so $c_{i+1} = 0$. $\blacksquare$

### 4.2 The MSB Ejection Lemma

**Lemma 4.4 (MSB Ejection).** *For any odd integer $n > 1$ with MSB at position $p$, the integer $3n+1$ has a $\mathtt{1}$-bit strictly above position $p$, separated from the remaining bits by at least one $\mathtt{0}$-bit.*

**Proof.** Since $n$ is odd, $b_0 = 1$ and $c_0 = 1$. The carry sequence begins at the LSB and propagates leftward. By Lemma 4.1, the carry passes through every cluster it reaches; by Lemma 4.3, it is absorbed only by gaps of width $\geq 2$.

Since $b_p = 1$ is the MSB, $b_{p+1} = 0$. There are two cases:

*Case 1: the carry reaches position $p$.* Then at position $p$: $b_{p-1} + b_p + c_p \geq 1 + 1 = 2$, so $c_{p+1} = 1$ and $r_p = (b_{p-1} + 1 + 1) \bmod 2$. At position $p+1$: $b_p + b_{p+1} + c_{p+1} = 1 + 0 + 1 = 2$, giving $r_{p+1} = 0$ and $c_{p+2} = 1$. The carry exits further up, eventually producing a $\mathtt{1}$-bit above position $p+1$ with a $\mathtt{0}$ at $p+1$.

*Case 2: the carry is absorbed below position $p$.* The addition $(n \ll 1) + n$ places the MSB of $n \ll 1$ at position $p+1$. Since $c_{p+1} = 0$ in this case, position $p+1$ receives $b_p + b_{p+1} + 0 = 1 + 0 + 0 = 1$, giving $r_{p+1} = 1$ with no further carry. Position $p$ receives $b_{p-1} + 1 + 0$; if $b_{p-1} = 1$ this produces $r_p = 0$ and $c_{p+1}' = 1$, giving the separator. If $b_{p-1} = 0$, then $r_p = 1$ and no carry, and the $\mathtt{1}$ at $p+1$ is isolated above position $p$.

In both cases a $\mathtt{1}$-bit appears strictly above position $p$ with a $\mathtt{0}$-separator below it. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 4.5.** *For all odd $n > 1$, $R(3n+1) < 1$. The fully-clustered state is unreachable as the output of any odd step.*

**Proof.** The isolated MSB guaranteed by Lemma 4.4 contributes $1$ to $H(3n+1)$ and $0$ to $C(3n+1)$. Therefore $C(3n+1) \leq H(3n+1) - 1$, giving $R(3n+1) \leq (H-1)/H < 1$. $\blacksquare$

### 4.3 The Exact Hamming Identity

**Lemma 4.6 (Hamming Identity).** *For any odd $n$ with bitlength $L$:*

$$H(3n+1) = 2H(n) + 1 - 2\sum_{i=1}^{L+2} c_i.$$

**Proof.** Sum the conservation law $(*)$ over all positions $i = 0, \ldots, L+1$:

$$\sum_i b_{i-1} + \sum_i b_i + \sum_i c_i = 2\sum_i c_{i+1} + \sum_i r_i.$$

The left side gives $H(n) + H(n) + \sum_i c_i$ (with $c_0 = 1$ included). Rearranging:

$$H(3n+1) = \sum_i r_i = 2H(n) + 1 - 2\sum_{i \geq 1} c_i. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark 4.7.** This identity is exact. The quantity $\sum c_i$ — the total carry weight — determines everything. High carry weight means high dissipation of $H$.

### 4.4 The Alternating String

**Proposition 4.8 (Alternating Collapse).** *Let $n = \mathtt{10101\cdots01}$ be an alternating bitstring of length $L$ (LSB $= 1$). Then $3n + 1 = 2^{L+1}$.*

**Proof.** Every $\mathtt{1}$-bit in $n$ is isolated: $b_j = 1$ implies $b_{j-1} = 0$. The initial carry $c_0 = 1$ enters position 0 ($b_0 = 1$): $0 + 1 + 1 = 2$, so $r_0 = 0$ and $c_1 = 1$. The carry then hits position 1 ($b_1 = 0$, $b_0 = 1$): $1 + 0 + 1 = 2$, so $r_1 = 0$ and $c_2 = 1$. Continuing: at every position the carry is forwarded and the output bit is $\mathtt{0}$. The carry exits above position $L-1$, producing a single $\mathtt{1}$ at position $L+1$.

Hence $3n+1 = 2^{L+1}$, with $H = 1$ and $R = 0$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 4.9.** *Alternating strings of length $L$ reach $2^{L+1}$ in one odd step, then descend to $1$ in $L+1$ further even steps: total $L + 2$ steps.*

Verification: $n = 21 = \mathtt{10101}_2 \Rightarrow 3 \times 21 + 1 = 64 = 2^6$. ✓

## 5. The Density-Connectivity Phase Space

We define two composite observables that together characterise the structural state of the system.

**Definition 5.1 (Density).** $T_H(n) = H(n)/L(n) \in (0, 1]$.

**Definition 5.2 (Clustering ratio).** $R(n) = C(n)/H(n) \in [0, 1)$ as before.

The joint state $(T_H, R) \in (0,1] \times [0,1)$ defines a **structural phase space** for the Collatz automaton. The dynamics in this space are:

| Branch | $L$ | $H$ | $C$ | $T_H$ | $R$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Even ($b_0 = 0$) | $-1$ | const | const | $\uparrow$ | const |
| Odd ($b_0 = 1$) | $+1$ (MSB ejection) | varies | varies | varies | $\downarrow$ (typically) |

The even branch is a **density pump**: it compresses volume while preserving structure. The odd branch is a **density regulator**: it injects structure (MSB ejection) while destroying internal connectivity (carry collapse). Together they produce a self-damping oscillator in the $(T_H, R)$ plane.

**Observation 5.3 (Forbidden corner).** By Corollary 4.5, the region $\{R = 1\}$ is forward-invariant under no orbit. The high-density, high-connectivity corner of phase space is unreachable as the output of any odd step.

## 6. The Clustering Ratio as a Candidate Lyapunov Observable

The MSB Ejection Lemma and the Hamming Identity together motivate the following conjecture.

**Conjecture 6.1 (Supermartingale Property).** *There exists $\lambda < 1$ such that for all odd $n > 1$:*

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mu_L}[R(T(n))] \leq \lambda \cdot \mathbb{E}_{\mu_L}[R(n)],$$

*where $\mu_L$ denotes the uniform measure on odd integers of bitlength $L$.*

A stronger pointwise version would assert:

$$R(T^k(n)) \to 0 \quad \text{as } k \to \infty$$

for every $n$, which combined with the sink-convergence of low-$R$, low-$T_H$ states would establish the conjecture.

Conjecture 6.1 is supported by computational evidence across bitlengths $L = 8$ to $48$ (see Section 7) and is consistent with the proved results of Section 4. Establishing it rigorously requires the programme of open problems described in Section 8.

---

## 7. Empirical Evidence

To test Conjecture 6.1, we sampled $N = 800$ odd integers uniformly at random for each bitlength $L \in \{8, 16, 24, 32, 48\}$ and computed $\Delta R = R(T(n)) - R(n)$ for each.

**Key findings:**

- The mean $\Delta R$ is negative for all tested values of $L$, ranging from $-0.08$ to $-0.14$.
- Approximately $65$–$70\%$ of individual steps decrease $R$.
- Odd steps show stronger $R$-reduction than even steps, consistent with Observation 3.2.
- The distribution of $\Delta R$ is left-skewed with a mode near $-0.1$.

The scatter plot of $R(n)$ versus $R(T(n))$ shows mass concentration below the diagonal $y = x$, confirming net dissipation of connectivity at the population level.

Alternating strings ($R = 0$) are confirmed to map to powers of $2$ in a single step, consistent with Proposition 4.8.

## 8. Open Problems

The following problems, if resolved, would constitute a complete proof of convergence via this framework.

**Problem 8.1 (Uniform carry lower bound).** Establish a tight lower bound for the carry sum $\sum c_i$ as a function of the cluster size distribution $\{k_j\}$ and gap widths $\{g_j\}$. The Hamming Identity (Lemma 4.6) reduces all of $H(3n+1)$ to this quantity; a sharp bound would yield explicit dissipation rates.

**Problem 8.2 (Cycle exclusion via $R$).** Prove that no periodic orbit of $T$ can sustain $R(T^k(n)) > \tau$ for all $k$, for some uniform $\tau > 0$. By Corollary 4.5, $R = 1$ is excluded; the question is whether $R$ can be sustained close to $1$ in a cycle.

**Problem 8.3 (Supermartingale bound).** Establish Conjecture 6.1 with an explicit $\lambda < 1$ and an explicit lower bound on the gap $1 - \lambda$ as a function of $L$.

**Problem 8.4 (Step bound).** Prove that every odd $n$ of bitlength $L$ reaches a power of $2$ within $2L - 1$ steps. Proposition 4.8 establishes this for alternating strings; the dense-cluster case ($H = \Theta(L)$) remains open.

**Problem 8.5 (Invariant measure).** Characterise the $T$-invariant measure on $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (the 2-adic integers). If this measure is supported on the cycle $\{1, 2, 4\}$, the conjecture follows. This is the deepest formulation of the problem and connects the present framework to Tao's ergodic-theoretic approach.

---

## 9. Conclusion

We have introduced a structural framework for the Collatz transformation based on three binary quantities: bit-length $L$, Hamming weight $H$, and cluster connectivity $C$. The clustering ratio $R = C/H$ provides a normalised measure of binary structural order that is preserved under even steps and systematically reduced by odd steps.

The main rigorous results are:

- **Lemma 4.1** (Carry-Through-Cluster): clusters are carry-transparent.
- **Lemma 4.4** (MSB Ejection): every odd step ejects an isolated $\mathtt{1}$ above the previous MSB.
- **Corollary 4.5**: the state $R = 1$ is unreachable; the system cannot sustain maximal clustering.
- **Lemma 4.6** (Hamming Identity): $H(3n+1) = 2H(n) + 1 - 2\sum c_i$ exactly.
- **Proposition 4.8**: alternating strings collapse to a power of $2$ in one odd step.

Together these establish that the Collatz transformation acts as a **deterministic dissipation mechanism** on binary cluster structure. The framework reduces the conjecture to a set of precise open problems (Section 8), the most tractable of which is a uniform lower bound on the carry sum for general cluster configurations.
